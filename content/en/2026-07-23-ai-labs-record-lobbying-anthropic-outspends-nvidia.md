The money U.S. AI companies pour into Washington has swelled to record levels. According to Q2 2026 (April–June) federal lobbying disclosures released on July 21, both Anthropic and OpenAI logged their largest quarterly lobbying outlays ever. Notably, Anthropic spent more on lobbying than chip giant Nvidia.

## Anthropic Sets a Record and Passes Nvidia

Anthropic's Q2 lobbying spend came to roughly $1.97M, a 26% jump from the prior quarter — its biggest quarterly figure since it started lobbying in March 2024. Its first-half 2026 total now tops $3.5M, already surpassing the roughly $3.1M it spent in all of 2025, and it did so in just six months.

The striking part is the reversal in scale. Anthropic outspent Nvidia this quarter and came close to Oracle's roughly $2M total. An AI startup that only began lobbying two years ago is now shoulder to shoulder with large hardware and infrastructure players on Washington spending.

## OpenAI Surges Too — $3.17M Combined

OpenAI, for its part, spent about $1.2M in Q2, up 18% from the previous quarter. Together the two companies laid out $3.17M from April through June, a 23% increase over Q1. Adding self-driving company Waymo, Bloomberg Government pegs the three firms' combined Q2 lobbying at roughly $4.3M — a new record.

## What the Money Is For

The disclosures show Anthropic's lobbying concentrated on a specific set of issues. Export controls, cybersecurity, and AI safety standards form the core, joined by Defense Department procurement, funding for the Energy Department's "Genesis Mission," and AI in health care.

This isn't just a bigger budget — it signals that AI regulation has entered the phase where actual rules get written. Frontier-model oversight, chip export controls, and access to government procurement are all on the table at once, and the labs want a voice where those rules are made.

## Why Now

Behind the surge is the intensifying push toward AI regulation in the U.S. With rules on safety standards, export controls, and government and defense procurement still being written, the labs whose business models hinge on those outcomes are moving early to secure influence. Another feature of this filing: while lobbying by legacy Big Tech and defense contractors is flat or slipping, AI labs' spending is climbing sharply.

<div class="article-callout tip">
<strong>Why it matters:</strong> Lobbying spend is a leading indicator of where a company feels regulatory risk. That Anthropic's and OpenAI's dollars cluster around export controls, safety standards, and government procurement suggests that's exactly where the AI regulatory front line will form over the next year or two.
</div>
