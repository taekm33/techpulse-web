Europe's largest open-weight AI company has raised the stakes again. On September 8, Mistral announced it had raised **€3 billion (about $3.5 billion)** in a Series D round led by Samsung Electronics. The round values the company at **more than €21 billion** post-money — what Mistral calls the largest equity fundraising round ever completed by a European technology company. The Paris-based firm reached the milestone roughly three years after opening its doors.

## A Samsung-led round, with European and U.S. backers

Samsung Electronics led the round as lead investor, joined by co-leads **Scaleup Europe Fund** (managed by EQT) and existing investor **PSG Equity**. New investors include **Advent**, funds and accounts managed by **BlackRock**, and the **Grand Duchy of Luxembourg**. Existing backers — **a16z, ASML, General Catalyst, Lightspeed, NVIDIA, and Salesforce Ventures** — also participated.

The jump in valuation is the headline. Mistral was valued at €11.7 billion in its September 2025 Series C, led by ASML. A year later, that figure has nearly doubled.

## Where the money goes: "compute we own"

CEO **Arthur Mensch** told CNBC the capital will go toward building and owning Mistral's own data centers, while renting additional computing capacity on top. He said Mistral intends to keep raising the share of compute it builds itself — so that the amount of compute the company owns grows by roughly 100% over the next five years.

That maps directly onto Mistral's **sovereign AI** pitch: rather than leaning on U.S. hyperscaler clouds for compute, the company wants to own and control the underlying infrastructure inside Europe.

<div class="article-callout info">
<strong>What "open-weight" means</strong> Model weights are released so companies can download, run, and fine-tune them on their own infrastructure. Because data never has to leave for an external API, the approach is especially attractive in a European market with strong regulatory and sovereignty demands.
</div>

## What it means

This round is less about a bigger price tag than about how seriously Europe is betting on its home-grown AI champion. Having an Asian conglomerate like Samsung lead — and a national government, Luxembourg, take part — suggests Mistral's sovereign-AI narrative is winning support from investors and states alike.

There's a paradox, though. Even as it champions sovereign AI, Mistral leans heavily on U.S. and Asian partners for capital, chips (NVIDIA), equipment (ASML), and now its lead investor (Samsung). Real independence will be settled by infrastructure ownership, not capital — and Mensch's "100% growth over five years" is the test that matters.
