Apple has closed the Tim Cook era. As telegraphed in its April 20 announcement, the company confirmed that John Ternus, senior vice president of Hardware Engineering, became chief executive officer effective September 1, 2026. Cook, who succeeded Steve Jobs in 2011, steps back after 15 years to serve as executive chairman of Apple's board. The board approved the move unanimously, and Apple described it as the result of "a thoughtful, long-term succession planning process."

## Who is taking over

Ternus joined Apple's product design team in 2001, became a vice president of Hardware Engineering in 2013, and joined the executive team as SVP in 2021. Over 25 years he oversaw hardware work across iPhone, Mac and Apple Watch, and helped introduce new categories like iPad and AirPods. This past fall's lineup—the iPhone 17 Pro and Pro Max, the thin-and-durable iPhone Air, and the iPhone 17—came out of his team, as did the recent MacBook Neo. Before Apple, he worked as a mechanical engineer at Virtual Research Systems and holds a bachelor's degree in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Pennsylvania.

At 15 years younger than Cook, Ternus was among the youngest of the executives long rumored as possible successors—a signal that Apple wants a leader for the long haul. In the announcement, Cook said Ternus "has the mind of an engineer, the soul of an innovator, and the heart to lead with integrity and with honor."

## Cook's 15 years, and his new role

Cook joined Apple in 1998 and became CEO in 2011, going on to launch new product categories including Apple Watch, AirPods and Apple Vision Pro, and services from iCloud and Apple Pay to Apple TV and Apple Music. During his tenure Apple's market capitalization rose more than 1,000%—from about $350 billion to $4 trillion—and annual revenue grew from $108 billion in fiscal 2011 to more than $416 billion in fiscal 2025, with Services alone now a $100-billion-plus business. As executive chairman, Cook will continue to assist with certain areas, including engaging with policymakers around the world. Arthur Levinson, non-executive chairman for the past 15 years, becomes lead independent director the same day, and Ternus joins the board of directors.

## Why now, and what's at stake

The handoff itself was long in the making, but the timing is delicate. Apple has been seen as trailing OpenAI, Google and Anthropic in generative AI, with questions hanging over the pace of its Siri overhaul and on-device AI strategy. An engineer-CEO's first test will be translating Apple's hardware strength into software and AI services that keep pace with rivals.
