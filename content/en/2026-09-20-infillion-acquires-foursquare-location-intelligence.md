Adtech platform Infillion announced on September 18 that it will acquire location intelligence company Foursquare. The target is advertising's hardest-to-measure last mile — whether an online ad actually led to a store visit or an offline purchase. Infillion plans to pair Foursquare's visitation data (more than 100 million points of interest across 200 countries, 16 billion human-verified check-ins, and coverage of 250 million U.S. devices) with the purchase intelligence of Catalina, which it already owns, connecting "ad → visit → purchase" on a single platform. Foursquare will keep operating as an independent brand within Infillion, and financial terms were not disclosed.
</div>

Infillion has bought one of the enduring names in location data. On September 18 in New York, the adtech company announced it will acquire the location intelligence platform Foursquare. At the center of the deal is a question the ad industry has failed to answer for decades: did my advertising actually get someone to walk into a store?

## Why Foursquare — seeing what "walled gardens" cannot

Measurement in digital advertising largely happens inside walled gardens. Platforms and retailer apps can only measure what happens within their own environments. Whether an online click led to an offline store visit sits outside those walls, and few players hold data at scale there. Foursquare is one of them.

Foursquare's footprint includes more than 100 million points of interest across 200 countries, 16 billion human-verified check-ins, and aggregated coverage of 250 million U.S. devices. It powers location measurement for hundreds of brands, serves all of the agency holding companies, and underpins place intelligence for major mobile apps and AI systems.

## Catalina + Foursquare = "from ad to outcome"

Infillion wanted Foursquare because it slots into pieces the company already owns. Infillion is an adtech platform assembled by unifying MediaMath, TrueX, Gimbal, Drawbridge, and Catalina, and it holds Catalina's purchase intelligence in particular. Add Foursquare's visitation data, and an advertiser can measure on one foundation everything from "who saw the ad" to "did they visit the store" to "did it turn into a purchase."

Rob Emrich, Founder and Executive Chairman of Infillion, said marketers have spent decades trying to prove that their advertising drives real-world outcomes, and that walled gardens and retailer apps can only measure what happens inside their own environments. Foursquare, he said, is one of the few places with the scaled data to measure what happens in the physical world; combined with Catalina purchase intelligence, Infillion becomes one of the few platforms that can accurately connect advertising to outcomes at national scale — "better inputs mean better outputs."

Gary Little, CEO of Foursquare, said that Foursquare joining Infillion "adds significant new real-world outcome data – all inside a platform that can serve customers at scale."

## Foursquare isn't going away — it stays an independent brand

Foursquare will continue to operate as an independent brand within Infillion and will keep selling its data to existing customers, including those using competing ad platforms, according to Axios. Axios reported that the acquisition will increase Infillion's revenue and headcount by roughly 20% to 25%, with the vast majority of Foursquare's roughly 150 employees joining Infillion's 650 full-time staff. Several members of Foursquare's leadership will join Infillion's Data Business Unit after the transaction closes.

<div class="article-callout tip">
The center of gravity here is measurement more than data. Tracing ad spend all the way to store visits and offline purchases has become a core industry problem as retail media has grown. But the more you combine location and purchase data, the larger the privacy questions. Infillion stressed a "privacy-safe" approach, and Foursquare has long pointed to its adherence to NAI and DAA self-regulatory principles — how those hold up in practice is the thing to watch.
</div>

## An open question — the fate of the consumer apps

Infillion said it will evaluate the future of Foursquare's consumer applications, Swarm and Superlocal, weighing a range of paths including open-source models and strategic partnerships. Where Foursquare's consumer services — which helped popularize location check-in culture — will fit inside a data- and measurement-focused company remains undecided.

## What it means

The Foursquare deal shows adtech's center of gravity shifting from "impressions" to "proof of outcomes." In an era when walled gardens surface only their own metrics, an independent measurement layer linking ads to offline results can be a differentiator for adtech players competing against the giants. Two things will decide it. One is privacy — whether location and purchase data can be combined while keeping regulators and consumers on side. The other is integration — whether Infillion, which has grown by buying and bolting together different technologies, can actually fold Foursquare into a single platform. Terms weren't disclosed, but what this deal is aiming at is the last mile of advertising measurement.
