Anthropic is in talks to acquire Israeli AI startup <strong>Decart</strong> for around <strong>$6 billion</strong>, Bloomberg reported on August 13. It would be Anthropic's largest-ever acquisition. Decart is known for real-time video and world models, but also for <strong>optimization software that improves how efficiently AI chips are used</strong> — and for an IPO-bound Anthropic, that compute-cost lever looks like the real prize. The deal is not finalized and could still fall through.
</div>

Anthropic, the maker of Claude, has entered talks to acquire Israeli startup Decart, with Bloomberg pegging the deal at roughly $6 billion on August 13. Nothing is signed, and the negotiation could collapse — but if it closes, it would be the largest acquisition Anthropic has publicly pursued. The timing matters: Anthropic is reported to be preparing an IPO, and this move reads as an effort to add scale and capabilities to its stack before going public.

## Who Decart is

Decart was founded in late 2023 by Dean Leitersdorf (CEO) and Moshe Shalev (CPO), both alumni of Israel's elite Unit 8200. It is often introduced as a "real-time video" company, but its products fall into three tracks.

| Product | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| DOS | AI optimization layer | Improves chip utilization, eases switching between chips |
| Lucy | Real-time interactive video world model | Live video transformation (e.g., virtual try-on) |
| Oasis | Physical-AI / simulation world model | Real-world simulation such as autonomous driving |

The piece to watch is not the flashy video demos but **DOS**. It is software that raises how efficiently chips are used during AI training and inference, and makes switching between different AI chips easier — in short, extracting more compute from the same hardware.

## Why Anthropic wants it

Like every frontier lab, Anthropic's tightest bottleneck is **compute**. With demand surging, getting more out of existing infrastructure matters as much as buying more GPUs. Decart's optimization work targets exactly that. It's why several reports frame cost reduction — rather than video-generation capability — as the primary motive behind the deal.

## From $4B to $6B in three months

Just three months earlier, in May 2026, Decart raised $300 million in a round led by Radical Ventures at a valuation near $4 billion. That round drew Nvidia, Toyota Ventures, Sequoia and Benchmark, along with individual backers including OpenAI co-founder Andrej Karpathy. The reported $6 billion figure is roughly a 50% jump in under three months. That Anthropic would buy a company Nvidia has backed also underscores how tangled today's AI-infrastructure map has become.

<div class="article-callout info">
<strong>Not final — negotiation stage</strong> This is an <em>ongoing negotiation</em> as reported by Bloomberg and others. Neither Anthropic nor Decart has confirmed it officially; the price and structure could change in a final agreement, or the deal could fall through entirely.
</div>

## What it means

Whether or not this particular deal closes, the signal is clear: the next battleground in frontier AI is not only model performance but **compute efficiency**. As the cost of scaling a model up one more notch grows exponentially, an optimization layer that delivers "the same chips, cheaper" becomes a strategic asset in its own right. If Anthropic is trying to buy both a new capability (video and world models) and a better cost structure right before an IPO, that's a move to strengthen its growth story and its margin story at the same time.
