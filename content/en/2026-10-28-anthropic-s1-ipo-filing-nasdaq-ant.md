<div class="article-tldr">
Anthropic, PBC <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec" target="_blank" rel="noopener">announced</a> on October 26, 2026 that it has publicly filed a Form S-1 with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. It plans to list Class A shares on the <strong>Nasdaq Global Select Market</strong> under <strong>ANT</strong>. According to the <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">S-1</a>, revenue for the six months ended June 30, 2026 was <strong>$16.286 billion</strong> (vs. $1.163 billion), and the operating loss was <strong>$693 million</strong> (vs. $2.135 billion). Share count, price and listing date are <strong>not yet determined</strong>, and no shares can be sold until the registration statement becomes effective.
</div>

This is an analysis of public materials: Anthropic's announcement, the S-1 as filed on SEC EDGAR, and press coverage. It is not investment advice, and TechPulse has no affiliation with Anthropic.

## Who this matters to

- **Companies and teams that run on Claude**: For the first time, the vendor's financials, governance and cloud dependence are disclosed in a regulated filing, which is useful for vendor-risk reviews.
- **Retail investors watching the listing**: The exchange and ticker are set, but there is no price range or date yet. It helps to separate what the filing confirms from what is speculation.
- **People working in AI**: This is a test case for how a frontier lab tries to lock in a safety mission through corporate structure while going public.

**Financials.** Per the <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">S-1</a>, first-half revenue rose roughly 14x year over year, while the operating loss shrank from $2.135 billion to $693 million. The $80.748 billion net loss reflects a $72.4 billion change in the fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, which the filing says was driven primarily by an increase in the fair value of the convertible notes. <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-officially-files-for-its-public-debut/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">TechCrunch</a> described the loss as "largely driven by noncash accounting adjustments tied to convertible debt." Anthropic's non-GAAP adjusted operating income was a positive $1.618 billion for the first half of 2026. The filing also warns that the company "may not achieve or sustain profitability in the future."

**Customers.** As of September 30, 2026, Anthropic reports more than 11,900 customers with $100,000+ in trailing-twelve-month revenue, more than 1,900 at $1 million+, and about 250 at $10 million+.

**Governance.** The filing describes five share classes.

| Class | Voting | Economic rights | Held by |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | One vote per share | Yes | IPO investors and others |
| N | None except as required by law | Yes | Converts to Class A on sale to an unaffiliated third party |
| T | Limited to electing Class T directors and certain matters | None | <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Long-Term Benefit Trust (LTBT)</a> |
| E | One vote per share on limited matters | See filing | Non-founder employees (none outstanding at the offering) |
| F | 50.1% of voting power on general matters | None | Founder LLC of seven co-founders |

The LTBT, described as five financially disinterested trustees, elects and removes the Class T directors, who will form a board majority. It has no day-to-day management or strategy authority. TechCrunch lists the Founder LLC members as Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah. Class A buyers get voting power well below their economic stake.

**Underwriters.** The cover lists Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley first, followed by a syndicate that includes J.P. Morgan and Citigroup. <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Anthropic's notice</a> names Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as sources for the preliminary prospectus.

**Risk factors.** The filing highlights dependence on staying at the frontier of model capability, reliance on cloud providers' operational execution (some of which build competing models), and the possibility that a default under certain commercial agreements could trigger cross-default provisions under its credit agreement. Total compute and infrastructure commitment amounts are left blank in this version.

## Not yet confirmed (kept separate from facts)

- **Offering size, price and date**: Anthropic says these "have not yet been determined." Any reported schedule is **unconfirmed** until an amended filing or official notice.
- **Valuation**: With no price range in the filing, an IPO valuation cannot be calculated yet. **Unconfirmed.**
- **Compute commitments**: The dollar figures for commitments through 2030 and for 2031–2045 are blank. They will likely appear in an amendment, but timing is **unconfirmed**.
- **Other reported disclosures**: Some outlets reported that the filing discusses a training pause and security incidents. We did not verify those passages in the filing ourselves, so we do not treat them as fact here.
- **Retail allocation**: Whether U.S. retail brokerages will offer IPO allocations is not stated in official materials. **Unconfirmed.**

## What it means for readers

For businesses that rely on Claude, the S-1 is due-diligence material. Revenue has grown fast and the operating loss has narrowed, but the company itself says profitability is not assured. Once public, Anthropic will have to file quarterly results and material events, which makes ongoing vendor monitoring easier.

For prospective shareholders, the structure limits influence. A majority of votes sits with the Founder LLC, and a majority of the board is chosen by the LTBT. That likely insulates product decisions such as pricing, safety policy and regional availability from short-term market pressure, but how it works in practice can only be judged after listing.

On timing: Nasdaq's regular session runs 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern. U.S. daylight saving time ends November 1, 2026, so a listing after that date would trade on EST. No first trading date has been announced.
