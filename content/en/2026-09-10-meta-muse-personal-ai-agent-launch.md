Meta unveiled Muse, a personal AI agent, on Tuesday, September 8. The key point is that Muse is an agentic product designed to take real actions on a user's behalf, not merely a chatbot that answers questions. In its official newsroom, Meta introduced Muse as "the world's first personal AI agent built for everyone," handling everything from triaging email to booking travel, filling out forms, negotiating, and completing payments.

## What It Does For You

Muse reads and organizes your inbox, books travel such as flights and hotels, and fills in online forms. It goes further by negotiating on your behalf and ultimately completing purchases with your registered card. All of this runs on a "dedicated secure cloud computer" that Meta assigns to Muse. The design moves beyond conversational chat toward accessing a user's accounts and payment methods to produce real-world outcomes.

## Pricing and Rollout

Meta paired a free tier with two paid subscriptions. The free tier is usable up to roughly 100 million tokens per week, while the paid options are "Power" at $20/month and "Maximum" at $100/month. The rollout starts in the US on iOS, Android and web, with support for Meta's AI glasses to follow. Muse runs on Meta's recently released Muse Spark model family.

| Tier | Monthly price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Free | ~100M tokens/week cap |
| Power | $20 | Expanded usage |
| Maximum | $100 | Maximum usage and performance |

## Privacy and Safety by Design

Because Muse touches your inbox and payment methods, Meta put privacy and safety front and center. Meta says there is no advertising inside the product. For payments, Muse uses Link by Stripe to generate a single-use card number, so a user's real payment details are not exposed to merchants. Running Muse in isolation on a dedicated secure cloud computer is a safeguard in the same spirit.

<div class="article-callout info">
As personal AI agents begin taking real actions across email, accounts and payments, risks such as prompt injection and misjudging irreversible actions become new focal points. As much as convenience, the design of permission scope and confirmation steps will determine how much users trust the product.
</div>

## The First Consumer Product From Superintelligence Labs

Muse is the first consumer-facing product from Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL), the group Meta built after hiring Alexandr Wang as its Chief AI Officer in 2025. Wang described Muse as an early but big step toward Meta's broader goal of "personal superintelligence." MSL had previously built momentum around the Muse Spark models and developer coding tools; this launch extends that work into a product for everyday users.

<div class="article-callout tip">
If ChatGPT and Claude opened the market with conversational interfaces, Muse is betting on an agent that acts on your behalf. How safely and accurately it handles consequential tasks like real payments and bookings will be decisive in the agent race against OpenAI, Anthropic and Google.
</div>

## Why It Matters

Muse shows that Big Tech's AI competition is shifting from "smarter models" to "agents that finish tasks for you." Because it handles sensitive permissions like email and payments, early trust and its safety track record will shape how fast it spreads. With OpenAI, Anthropic and Google each pushing their own agent products, the next thing to watch is what impact Meta — with its multibillion-user base — can make in the consumer market.
