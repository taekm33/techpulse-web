<div class="article-tldr">
LawZero, the nonprofit founded by Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio, has secured a joint commitment of up to CAD 300 million from the governments of Canada and Germany. Canada is contributing CAD 150 million and Germany EUR 100 million. The funding backs "Scientist AI," a safe-by-design methodology for systems that reason transparently from facts and do not pursue goals of their own. Announced Sept 16 at the ALL IN conference in Montréal by ministers from both countries, it stands out as a government-led bet on an alternative to the overheating race toward autonomous AI agents.
</div>

At the ALL IN conference in Montréal on September 16, the governments of Canada and Germany announced plans to invest up to CAD 300 million in LawZero, the nonprofit AI-safety organization founded by Yoshua Bengio. Canada will provide CAD 150 million and Germany EUR 100 million. Canada's Evan Solomon, Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation, and Germany's Dr. Karsten Wildberger, Federal Minister for Digital Transformation and Government Modernisation, unveiled the commitment together.

## What it funds — 'Scientist AI'

At the core is LawZero's methodology, Scientist AI. Unlike most frontier systems today, which are designed to act autonomously or pursue objectives, Scientist AI is built to reason transparently and produce reliable, evidence-based outputs without being biased by goals of its own. Bengio has described the approach as working "purely on the basis of facts — like an objective scientist" and not pursuing its own agenda. In effect, it is a government-funded push for an alternative architecture to the autonomous agents whose potential uncontrollability has drawn growing concern.

LawZero plans to begin with tools that assess and oversee existing AI systems and support scientific research, then advance toward a full methodology for safe-by-design frontier models.

## Why Canada and Germany — 'sovereign AI'

The investment reads as more than research money; it is a technological-sovereignty play. Canada's share, through its Strategic Response Fund, supports research and engineering talent plus computing capacity, and the project is set to create 360 full-time jobs in Canada and stand up a dedicated sovereign computing infrastructure in partnership with Canadian firms Hypertec and 5C. By anchoring critical research, talent and compute domestically, Ottawa aims to reduce reliance on foreign-controlled AI systems.

Germany's funding is subject to notification by the European Commission and will support R&D at LawZero's new German office, ensuring European AI norms and regulations are baked in from the project's outset.

<div class="article-callout tip">
The announcement follows the AI Joint Declaration of Intent and the Sovereign Technology Alliance that Canada and Germany launched in February. It signals that both countries' strategic push to reduce dependence on U.S. and Chinese big-tech models is now translating into concrete funding.
</div>

## Bengio's message — 'safety is a core capability'

Bengio framed the backing bluntly: "As AI risks multiply and accelerate, our priority must be building solutions to make this technology safe and providing alternative models people can genuinely trust," adding that "recent events demonstrate that safety is itself a core capability." The comments fit the deep-learning pioneer's repeated warnings about the loss-of-control risks posed by autonomous AI.

Wildberger called the effort "a novel, mathematically grounded approach: to develop an inherently safe AI," describing the safe-by-design model as "an alternative to potentially uncontrollable autonomous AI agents."

## What it means

The defining feature here is that public money is going directly into a safety-first alternative architecture rather than the commercial frontier race. The open question is whether a non-agentic approach like Scientist AI can become a practical, widely used tool in a market dominated by performance and cost. At the same time, the Canada–Germany "sovereign AI" move shows the geopolitics of frontier models diversifying beyond the U.S.–China duopoly.
