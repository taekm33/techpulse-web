<div class="article-tldr">
On September 16, Anthropic announced a unified interface that merges Claude's ordinary chat with Cowork, its agentic workspace for bigger, multi-step jobs. Users no longer switch tabs to choose a task type; Claude gauges the size of a request and decides whether it needs a quick answer, a document, a presentation, a design, or a longer agentic run. Alongside it, the company launched Claude Docs and Claude Slides in beta. Together the moves push Anthropic squarely into the workplace software market long dominated by Google Workspace and Microsoft 365.
</div>

Anthropic is changing how people use Claude. Cowork—the separate, agentic space for handing off large, multi-step work—is being folded into the same window as regular chat. The company calls the result "One Claude." Starting September 16, Claude sizes up a request on its own and pulls in the capabilities that were previously Cowork-only from inside a normal conversation.

## Why merge — "deciding where to work was the friction"

Anthropic originally built Cowork as a place for bigger tasks, but said users hit friction at the very first step: figuring out whether a job belonged in chat or in Cowork. The merger removes that choice. You simply describe what you need, and the product decides—whether the job is a single answer, a document, a deck, a visual design, or a long-running agentic process. The interface gets simpler even as the underlying system takes on more complex work.

## What's new — Docs, Slides, and Design in any chat

The substance of the announcement sits in two new products. **Claude Docs** is a collaborative document tool for co-writing, editing, and sharing documents inside a conversation. **Claude Slides** is a presentation tool for building and even presenting decks. Both launch in beta. On top of that, **Claude Design**—introduced in April for websites and prototypes—can now be invoked from any ordinary chat to produce visual outputs like slides and prototypes.

## Formats and export

Docs and Slides are built to hand results off into real workflows. Documents and presentations work with Google Docs, Microsoft Word, PowerPoint, and PDF formats, and slides can be downloaded as PDF or PowerPoint files or shared by link. The intent is to keep drafting, refining, and delivering to a team inside one continuous flow.

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| Claude Docs | Live, collaborative document writing and editing in chat |
| Claude Slides | Build and present decks; export to PDF/PPT; share by link |
| Claude Design | Generate slides, prototypes, and visuals from any chat |
| Unified routing | Auto-routes a request to answer / doc / deck / agent |

<div class="article-callout tip">
Collapsing separate tabs into one surface follows a broader design trend: add capability while cutting the burden of choosing. The catch is that automatic routing lets the product decide what to build—so when the output doesn't match intent, how easily a user can override or redirect it will shape real-world satisfaction.
</div>

## How far it reaches — the rollout

This is not a full open release. The unified experience rolls out first to Claude Pro and Max subscribers across web, desktop, and mobile over the coming weeks, with Team and Free plans to follow. Enterprise administrators get at least 30 days' notice before changes reach their organizations. Between the beta status of the new products and the higher tiers going first, it will take time before every user feels the change.

## What it means

The overhaul signals Anthropic stretching from a model provider toward a workplace software provider. Creating documents, decks, and designs inside a conversation—then exporting to files and sharing by link—overlaps directly with the ground Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 (Copilot) have held. The real test is stickiness: only when users finish not just drafts but final versions inside Claude, and hand them to collaborators, will the merger and the new tools translate into share of the market.
