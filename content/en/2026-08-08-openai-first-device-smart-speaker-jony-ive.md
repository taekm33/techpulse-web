OpenAI's push beyond software into consumer hardware is coming into focus. In an Aug 6 report, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman said the company's first device will take the form of a smart speaker without a display. Rather than an ambitious phone-replacing form factor, it sounds closer to an "ambient" gadget you place around the house and talk to.

## What the reports describe

The defining detail across outlets is the absence of a screen. The device is said to be a rounded object roughly the size of a hockey puck, engineered to be picked up with one hand and moved from room to room. It carries a built-in battery so it isn't tethered to an outlet, along with a camera, an array of sensors, and ambient lighting.

The most striking element is the "moving parts." Instead of a static box, the speaker is reported to shift subtly as it responds, so that voice conversations feel less mechanical. That is a physical expression of the "humanlike interactivity" OpenAI has repeatedly emphasized.

## The Jony Ive factor

Jony Ive sits at the center of this project. The designer behind the iPhone and Apple Watch left Apple in 2019 to found the design studio LoveFrom, and later started the AI hardware startup io. OpenAI absorbed that team in 2025 through an all-stock deal valued at roughly $6.5 billion, effectively making Ive's group its hardware brain. This smart speaker looks poised to be the first product of that acquisition.

On the manufacturing side, some reports point to Luxshare — a core Apple supply-chain partner — handling production. In other words, Apple-adjacent talent and partners are lined up on both design and mass production.

<div class="article-callout tip">
A screenless AI speaker may look like Amazon Echo or Apple HomePod on the surface, but the ambition differs. Rather than a command handler for weather and timers, it aims to be a companion device that keeps ChatGPT-grade conversational AI constantly within reach.
</div>

## Open questions

It is worth stressing that none of this is an official announcement. Specs, price and timing are all report-based expectations, and OpenAI has not finalized or detailed the product publicly. The privacy questions raised by a camera-equipped, always-sensing device entering the home, whether a $300-plus price can win over buyers, and how the competitive landscape shifts by 2027 all remain open.

What is clear is the direction: OpenAI wants to widen its user touchpoints beyond apps and APIs into physical hardware. The strategy reads less like replacing the smartphone and more like adding one more always-on AI touchpoint alongside it.
